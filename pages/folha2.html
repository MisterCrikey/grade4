A Reef in Trouble: Ocean Heatwaves Harm Coral in Brazil’s Abrolhos

An 18-year study found that fire coral has nearly disappeared. Another coral, the Bahia brain coral, fell by 45%.

WhereAbrolhos, southern Bahia, Brazil
When2 March 2026
AuthorJéssica Maes (São Paulo)
SourceFolha de S.Paulo (English edition, Science and Health)

ABROLHOS, Brazil — The coral reefs of Abrolhos, in southern Bahia, are in trouble. An 18-year study found that some corals have lost many of their numbers. One of them, fire coral, has nearly disappeared. The article names climate change, pollution and deforestation as causes of harm to the reefs.

Past & Present: Comparing the Ecosystem

Corals are small sea animals that build reefs. The Abrolhos Bank is a large area of sea along Brazil’s coast. It covers 46,000 square kilometers. It has the largest and most diverse coral reefs in the South Atlantic. Diverse means many different kinds. The article says some of its coral species are essential to the ecological balance of the South Atlantic. Ecological balance means living things in nature depend on each other.

Two corals are named in the study:

  • Fire coral. It is found only in Brazil. It is a branching coral, shaped like a small tree. Branching corals serve as nurseries for fish and invertebrates. A nursery is a safe place where young animals grow. Invertebrates are animals without a backbone.
  • Bahia brain coral. It is an endemic species. Endemic means found only in one region.
“Drastic population declines.”Folha de S.Paulo, describing the study’s findings

Then: The region was historically less affected by thermal stress. Thermal stress means stress from heat. When the study began, fire coral already covered less than 2% of the area.

Now: The region has had more marine heatwaves since 2010. A marine heatwave is a time when sea water stays much hotter than usual. Fire coral is now “in a state of collapse” in the region. Bahia brain coral fell by 45%. A photo caption names a “weed” coral. It is a species that takes over reefs after more complex species die.

Human Impact: What Changed and Why?

The article says the reefs face severe impacts from climate change, pollution and deforestation. A subheading adds that ocean heatwaves combined with pollution and local deforestation are harming reef health. Deforestation means cutting down or clearing forest.

The article also describes what scientists did. They studied monitoring data from 2006 to 2023. They published the study in the journal Proceedings of the Royal Society.

Investigator’s note

The article does not say what kind of pollution reaches the reef. It does not say where the deforestation happens. It also does not explain how people’s actions on land can harm a coral reef.

Research question: How can pollution or cutting down trees on land harm the sea? Use another source.

Climate Change & Consequences

The article names climate change as one cause of harm to the reefs. It gives this timeline:

  • Since 2010, marine heatwaves in the region have been increasing.
  • A marine heatwave began in 2016. It peaked in 2017.
  • That heatwave triggered the third global bleaching event.
  • High temperatures in 2017 also caused a sharp drop in Bahia brain coral.

The study period (2006–2023) had three mass bleaching events. Two were global. One was local. The article does not explain bleaching. It does link bleaching events to very hot water.

“Now in a state of collapse.”Folha de S.Paulo, describing fire coral in Abrolhos

Consequences for nature: Fire coral nearly disappeared. Bahia brain coral fell by 45%. Fire coral is a nursery for fish and invertebrates. The article does not say what this means for those animals.
