Unilever Increases Climate Action with Mangrove Restoration

By Chloe Williment
August 26, 2026
7 mins
Share this article
Prioritise Us on Google
Share this article
Prioritise Us on Google
Unilever and Conservation International are scaling mangrove restoration in Ecuador, with Rianne Buter highlighting the role of nature in climate action

Mangroves are becoming an increasingly important nature-based solution as climate risks intensify across coastal regions.

In Ecuador, Conservation International Ecuador and Unilever are joining forces on Living Gulf: Mangroves for People, Climate and Nature, a five-year initiative focused on protecting and restoring mangrove ecosystems.

The programme aims to conserve at least 30,000 hectares and restore a further 2,600 hectares across the Gulf of Guayaquil.

It forms part of a wider effort involving public and private partners to strengthen biodiversity, climate resilience and coastal livelihoods.

Protecting Ecuador’s mangroves

Mangroves cover more than half of Ecuador’s coastline and support fisheries and the livelihoods of hundreds of thousands of families. 

However, almost 25% of the country’s mangroves have been lost over the past 50 years, largely due to the expansion of shrimp farming.

The Gulf of Guayaquil contains approximately 80% of Ecuador’s mangroves and holding a carbon reserve of more than 440 million tonnes. 

Living Gulf plans to operate across eight municipalities that depend on mangroves, these include:

  • Arenillas
  • El Guabo
  • Machala
  • Santa Rosa
  • Guayaquil
  • Durán
  • Balao
  • Naranjal.

The programme will establish 15 mangrove nurseries capable of producing approximately 1.2 million seedlings.

It will also work towards conserving more than 4.8 million tonnes of irrecoverable carbon stored within these ecosystems.

“Unilever has contributed to the World Business Council for Sustainable Development's paper submitted to the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification today, highlighting what is needed to unlock more investment in nature,” writes Rianne Buter, Global Vice President of Sustainability at Unilever, on LinkedIn.

“A great example of this in action is our partnership with Conservation International through Unilever's Climate & Nature Fund: Living Gulf: Mangroves for People, Climate and Nature.

“Together, we're working to protect at least 30,000 hectares of mangroves and restore a further 2,600 hectares in Ecuador. 

“The project is expected to benefit around 3,000 people in coastal communities while helping tackle climate change and protect biodiversity.”

Putting communities at the centre

Community participation is central to the initiative, with Living Gulf designed to strengthen the organisations and people already managing Ecuador’s mangroves. 

The programme will support Sustainable Use and Custodianship Agreements and expand access to incentives such as Socio Manglar, linking private investment with existing public policies. 

Women’s leadership will also be a focus, with plans to train 300 women, support community enterprises and create 350 local jobs. 

The approach positions local communities as participants in conservation rather than simply beneficiaries of investment. 

“At the heart of Living Gulf are the communities who have protected mangroves for generations,” says Carolina Rosero, Vice President and Executive Director, Conservation International Ecuador. 

“By strengthening their leadership, providing resources and supporting sustainable practices, this initiative elevates them as key leaders and partners in delivering nature-based solutions that safeguard ecosystems, store carbon and build resilient coastal futures. 

Scaling nature investment

Unilever’s involvement in Living Gulf also helped unlock larger-scale public financing through the Green Climate Fund, in coordination with the Government of Ecuador. 

This financing has enabled the Mangroves for Climate project, led by Conservation International Ecuador in partnership with Ecuador’s Ministry of Environment and Energy. 

Together, the complementary initiatives are expected to have an impact across more than 130,000 hectares of mangroves nationwide. 

The partnership reflects Unilever’s broader approach to nature, where protecting and restoring ecosystems is linked to supply chain resilience, climate action and livelihoods. 

“At Unilever, we believe in a growth model that creates shared value, not only for the business, but also for society, communities and the environment,” says Marcos Dueñas, General Manager of Unilever Ecuador. 

“This partnership represents much more than a one-time contribution: it is our way of being present in the country and of investing in Ecuador, its talent and its ability to develop solutions that matter to the world.”

The company says natural and agricultural ecosystems are critical to its business, with biodiversity loss, soil degradation and water scarcity creating risks for crop yields, raw material availability and supply chain continuity.

A wider nature and climate strategy

Living Gulf sits alongside Unilever’s wider target to help protect and restore one million hectares of natural ecosystems by 2030.

By the end of 2025, the company said it had helped protect and restore 660,000 hectares since 2021, with the Conservation International partnership in Ecuador among three new programmes added during the year.

Unilever is also targeting one million hectares under regenerative agriculture practices by 2030 and has implemented 254,000 hectares across 34 programmes in 17 countries since 2021.

Its nature strategy includes deforestation-free sourcing, sustainable agriculture, ecosystem restoration, water stewardship and partnerships designed to scale action beyond its own operations.

The company says its nature work also creates benefits for climate and livelihoods, including greater resilience for farmers and supply chains.

Unilever is aiming to make the biggest impact in six main ways:
  • Maintaining deforestation-free supply chains
  • Taking a regenerative approach to farming
  • Improving water security in water-stressed areas
  • Sourcing ingredients sustainably
  • Implementing nature protection and restoration
  • Working with others to unlock nature actions at scale

Unilever’s partners and projects

PepsiCo: Unilever is working pre-competitively with PepsiCo and other companies in the US to support farmers adopting more sustainable agricultural practices. The collaboration focuses on improving access to tools, training and funding. It forms part of Unilever’s broader regenerative agriculture strategy, which seeks to address climate, soil and biodiversity impacts while strengthening agricultural resilience.

Axa and Tikehau Capital: Unilever has joined forces with finance partners Axa and Tikehau Capital to establish a private equity fund designed to scale regenerative agriculture globally. The initiative reflects the company’s focus on using partnerships and investment to expand nature-positive agricultural practices. It complements Unilever’s target to implement regenerative agriculture across one million hectares by 2030.

Business for Nature: Unilever is part of Business for Nature, alongside more than 100 businesses and financial institutions calling for stronger government action on nature. The collaboration focuses on encouraging governments to implement and enforce ambitious policies addressing nature loss. For Unilever, policy engagement complements its operational work on biodiversity, sustainable sourcing, deforestation and ecosystem restoration.

RSPO and palm oil coalitions: Unilever participates in industry groups including the Roundtable on Sustainable Palm Oil, the Consumer Goods Forum’s Forest Positive Coalition, the Palm Oil Collaboration Group and the Malaysian Sustainable Palm Oil Standard Impact Alliance. These partnerships support industry-wide efforts on sustainable palm oil, deforestation and forest protection, while enabling Unilever to share experience in traceability, verification and smallholder engagement.

Nature Metrics and Biome Makers: Unilever is working with Nature Metrics and Biome Makers to measure environmental outcomes from regenerative agriculture programmes. Their work uses environmental DNA sampling to assess changes in soil and insect biodiversity across Unilever’s supply chain. The partnerships are intended to improve how Unilever measures nature-related outcomes as it expands regenerative farming initiatives across sourcing landscapes.

Rothamsted Research – soil carbon: Unilever is working with Rothamsted Research to develop robust approaches for soil organic carbon sampling and modelling. The partnership supports more accurate measurement of carbon removals from regenerative agriculture programmes. This work forms part of Unilever’s broader effort to measure environmental outcomes while scaling agricultural practices such as cover cropping, crop rotation and reduced tillage.

WBCSD Landscape Accelerator Brazil: Through the World Business Council for Sustainable Development’s Landscape Accelerator Brazil, Unilever is supporting policy and finance solutions for the regenerative transformation of the Cerrado. The landscape is a key sourcing region for the company’s soybean oil. The initiative links regenerative agriculture with broader landscape-level action, recognising that farmers need supportive policy and financing to accelerate transformation.
