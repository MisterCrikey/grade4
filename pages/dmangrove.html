What Is a Mangrove?

Trees that stand in the sea

A mangrove 🌿 is a tree that grows where a river meets the salty sea 🌊. It has shiny green leaves on top and a tangle of roots below. The roots look like long skinny legs standing in the water.

Living in salty mud

Mangroves have a tough life. Twice a day the tide 🌙 comes in and covers part of the tree. Then the tide goes out and leaves thick, sticky mud. Their bark is spongy and a little hairy, and it helps protect them from muddy, salty water.

At low tide, the mud can smell like rotten eggs 🥚. That smell comes from gas made by old plants and animals breaking down in the mud. Smelly mud is a sign of a busy, living forest.

Why mangroves matter

Mangroves do many jobs. Their roots catch dirt that could cover and hurt coral reefs 🪸. They soak up extra food from the water, so the water stays balanced. They also help stop the shore from washing away in storms.

Mangroves are a nursery too. Baby fish 🐟 and little sharks 🦈 hide in the tangled roots, where big hunters can’t easily reach them. Mangroves also store carbon, which is a gas that can warm our planet. About one out of every ten mangrove forests in the world is in Brazil 🇧🇷.

Crabs everywhere

If you walk in a mangrove forest, you will hear crabs 🦀 scuttling away. A fisherman named Genilson knows them all. The little red ones that climb trees are used as bait. The big yellow crab with strong claws is called uçá. There are also blue crabs and tiny fiddler crabs.

People of the mangrove

In southern Brazil, there is a protected place called the Cassurubá Reserve. About 300 families live in villages there. They are called river people. They can catch crabs and shellfish, but they must do it carefully.

Genilson started following his father and grandfather into the mangrove when he was only five. He learned to read the tides and the moon. He also learned which months to leave crabs alone so they can have babies.

Mangroves in danger

Mangroves are in danger in many places. People cut them down to make shrimp 🦐 farms, hotels and houses. Trash like old fishing nets can trap animals. Oceans are also rising, so mangroves need dry salty flats nearby where they can move back. If those flats get built on, the trees have nowhere to go.

The river people did not want to lose their forest. They worked with the government for years, and in 2009 the reserve was made official. Now they help take care of it.

A new way to help

Living in the reserve is not easy. There is no electricity, no running water and no clinic. Many young people move to cities.

So the villages started a small tourism 📸 project. Visitors walk in the mangroves, eat local food and learn from the guides, who get paid. Only about 30 visitors come each month, which keeps the forest safe. The villagers hope it will bring a dock and electricity.

Genilson says he cannot be separated from the mangrove. To him, taking care of the mangrove means taking care of the people who work in it.
