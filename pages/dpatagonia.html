Rivers of Ice: The Glaciers of Patagonia

What is a glacier?

A glacier 🧊 is a giant river of ice. It starts as snow ❄️ high on a mountain. Year after year, new snow piles on top and squeezes the old snow into ice. The heavy ice slowly slides downhill.

The big ice field

Patagonia 🏔️ is at the bottom of South America. It has the biggest mass of ice in the Southern Hemisphere, except for Antarctica. It is called the Patagonian Ice Field. It stretches about 350 kilometers from north to south.

Wet winds from the Pacific Ocean 🌊 drop snow on the mountains all the time. The glaciers flow west into the fjords of Chile 🇨🇱. They also flow east into big lakes in Argentina 🇦🇷.

Glaciers and history

Glaciers have been in Patagonia for thousands of years. Long ago, the Tehuelche people lived in this region. Their name for one tall mountain, El Chaltén, means “smoking cloud.” Visitors from far away came much later.

In 1833, the scientist Charles Darwin 📖 sailed through the Beagle Channel at the bottom of the continent. He thought the blue glaciers were beautiful. He said the icebergs floating in the water looked like a tiny polar sea.

Who was Perito Moreno?

The most famous glacier in Patagonia is named after a man, Francisco Moreno 🧭. He explored the region in the 1870s. In Spanish, “perito” means expert. Moreno was an expert at drawing maps 🗺️ and helped Argentina and Chile agree on where their border should be.

In 1879, a Chilean navy captain, Juan Tomás Rogers, was the first non-native person to see the glacier. He gave it a different name, after a Chilean naval officer. In 1899, an Argentine officer renamed it Perito Moreno, to honor the explorer.

Protecting the ice

In 1937, Argentina made Los Glaciares National Park 🏞️ to protect this wild land of ice, lakes and mountains. Then in 1981, the United Nations group UNESCO named it a World Heritage Site. That means it is special for the whole world.

Famous glaciers

Perito Moreno has a bright blue front that pokes into a lake. Upsala is even bigger. Spegazzini has an ice wall more than 130 meters tall, as high as a 40-floor building. Grey Glacier in Chile is about 14 kilometers long. The Tronador glacier is named “thunder” ⚡ because of the loud crash when ice falls.

Exploring glaciers

Today, people explore glaciers by boat, kayak and on foot 🥾. Guides help hikers put on spiked boots called crampons. They grip the ice so hikers can walk on top of a glacier safely. Some glaciers can only be seen from a ship.

A warming world

Glaciers are in trouble. The Earth is getting warmer 🌡️, and most glaciers in Patagonia are melting back. The ice melts faster than it used to. In Chile, all but two of its 26,000 glaciers are shrinking.

There is one surprise. Perito Moreno has stayed steady for many years, which makes it special. Scientists keep watching it, and all the other glaciers, to learn how we can protect them.
