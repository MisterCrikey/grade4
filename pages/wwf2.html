Saving the Restinga: A New Protected Area for São Paulo’s Coast

A proposed park of 8,025 hectares in Bertioga could protect one of the most threatened parts of the Atlantic Forest.

WhereBertioga, São Paulo State, Brazil
WhenFebruary 4, 2010
AuthorNot listed (published by WWF-Brasil)
SourceWWF Brasil (wwf.org.br)

One of the most important areas of Atlantic Forest in São Paulo state may soon get a new protected area. The proposed area has 8,025 hectares and is in the municipality of Bertioga, on the central coast.

Past & Present: Comparing the Ecosystem

This place is part of the São Paulo restinga. Restinga formations stretch along almost the whole coast of Brazil. They are a mix of plants and animals that have adapted to the sea and to sandy soil. The article says the restinga is the most threatened area of Atlantic Forest in São Paulo state.

Scientists studied an area of 10,393 hectares called the Bertioga polygon. They found rich biodiversity:

  • About 1,000 plant species. That is 13% of all the plant species in the whole state.
  • 40 species of amphibians and 53 species of reptiles.
  • 25 species of large and medium-sized mammals.
  • Many orchid species, and many threatened bird species.

Two places in the region have the most species in São Paulo state. They have the greatest variety of amphibians known for this kind of biome. Of the plants found, 44 are threatened with extinction. Another 17 types of vegetation are endemic: they exist only in the states of Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo.

Human Impact: What Changed and Why?

The article says the restinga is “vulnerable to pressure from real estate interests and tourism.” It also says the remaining stands of forest “continue to suffer great pressure.”

The article also describes how people have chosen parks in the past. Luciana Simões, coordinator of WWF-Brasil’s Atlantic Forest Programme, says most parks were created because of certain opportunities, not because of a plan to protect nature.

“We want to get away from opportunism and the old ways of creating parks in a random manner.”Luciana Simões, Atlantic Forest Programme coordinator

This time, the choice was made with technical criteria. These included the animals, the plants and the water resources of the place. The article calls this a pilot process to test criteria for choosing parks. The goal is to protect the habitats that best represent the area, with no gaps for plants, animals and natural processes.

Climate Change & Consequences

Investigator’s note: This article does not mention climate change. Instead, it explains what could be lost when nature is under pressure. Use another source to research how climate change might affect a coastal forest.

The article names several things at risk. The area protects rivers that give water to the region. These rivers form three sub-basins: Una, Itaguaré and Guaratuba. It is also home to threatened species that live only in this biome. Six mammal species are threatened with extinction. The low forests near the sea, from 0 to 50 metres above sea level, are an Important Bird Area. According to Birdlife International / SAVE Brasil, such places are critically important for the long-term conservation of birds.

Solutions & Shared Responsibility

The solution in this article is a new protected area. It would be the first to safeguard part of the plain at the bottom of the Serra do Mar State Park mountains. The area would also work as a corridor for wildlife, connecting to the highlands. It would protect two river sub-basins, Itaguaré and Guaratuba.

WWF-Brasil’s Atlantic Forest Programme did the studies with two partners: the Forest Foundation and the São Paulo Forestry Institute. Many kinds of researchers worked on them.

People are also part of the process. From December 4 to 7, 2009, four public meetings were held. A public hearing was expected in February 2010. Simões says protected areas must find “harmony between human beings and the environment,” and that this harmony means a better quality of life for people.

If people take action

  • 8,025 hectares could be protected.
  • Rivers that supply the region’s water would be protected.
  • Threatened plants, mammals and birds would get a safer home.
  • It would be the first area to protect part of this plain.

If nothing is done

The article does not say. Its clues: the remaining forest is under great pressure, and only one protected area covers this habitat in the state system. What do you predict? Write it in your journal and call it a prediction.
