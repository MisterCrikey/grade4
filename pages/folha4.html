A Reef in Trouble: Ocean Heatwaves Harm Coral in Brazil’s Abrolhos

An 18-year study found that fire coral has nearly disappeared. Another coral, the Bahia brain coral, fell by 45%.

WhereAbrolhos, southern Bahia, Brazil
When2 March 2026
AuthorJéssica Maes (São Paulo)
SourceFolha de S.Paulo (English edition, Science and Health)

ABROLHOS, Brazil — The coral reefs of Abrolhos, in southern Bahia, are in trouble. An 18-year study found that some corals have lost many of their numbers. One of them, fire coral, has nearly disappeared. Climate change, pollution and deforestation are named as causes of harm to the reefs.

Past & Present: Comparing the Ecosystem

Corals are small sea animals that build reefs. The Abrolhos Bank is a large area of sea along Brazil’s coast. It covers 46,000 square kilometers. It has the largest and most diverse coral reefs in the South Atlantic. Diverse means many different kinds. Some of its coral species are essential to the ecological balance of the South Atlantic. Ecological balance means living things in nature depend on each other.

Two corals are named in the study:

  • Fire coral. It is found only in Brazil. It is a branching coral, shaped like a small tree. Branching corals serve as nurseries for fish and invertebrates. A nursery is a safe place where young animals grow. Invertebrates are animals without a backbone.
  • Bahia brain coral. It is an endemic species. Endemic means found only in one region.
“Drastic population declines.”Folha de S.Paulo, describing the study’s findings

Then: The region was historically less affected by thermal stress. Thermal stress means stress from heat. When the study began, fire coral already covered less than 2% of the area.

Now: The region has had more marine heatwaves since 2010. A marine heatwave is a time when sea water stays much hotter than usual. Fire coral is now “in a state of collapse” in the region. Bahia brain coral fell by 45%. There is also a “weed” coral. It is a species that takes over reefs after more complex species die.

Who lives on the reef? Many animals live in and around Abrolhos. The Abrolhos Marine National Park has about 266 kinds of fish, more than 500 kinds of crustaceans (animals like crabs and shrimp), and about 293 kinds of mollusks (animals like snails). Big fish called groupers, sea turtles and sea birds live there too. More than 90 kinds of coral grow in the park. Every year, from July to November, humpback whales come to Abrolhos to give birth and care for their babies.

Human Impact: What Changed and Why?

The reefs face severe impacts from climate change, pollution and deforestation. Ocean heatwaves combined with pollution and local deforestation are harming reef health. Deforestation means cutting down or clearing forest.

Here is what scientists did. They studied monitoring data from 2006 to 2023. They published the study in the journal Proceedings of the Royal Society.

How can land actions harm the sea? Rain and rivers carry things from the land to the sea. When land is cleared, extra soil, called sediment, can wash into rivers and then into the ocean. Sediment makes the water cloudy, so less sunlight reaches the corals. It can also settle on corals like a blanket, which makes it hard for them to eat, grow and have babies. Farm fertilizer and sewage can add too many nutrients to the water. Then algae can grow too much and crowd out the coral. Chemicals like pesticides can also harm coral.

Climate Change & Consequences

Climate change is one cause of harm to the reefs. Here is the timeline:

  • Since 2010, marine heatwaves in the region have been increasing.
  • A marine heatwave began in 2016. It peaked in 2017.
  • That heatwave triggered the third global bleaching event.
  • High temperatures in 2017 also caused a sharp drop in Bahia brain coral.

The study period (2006–2023) had three mass bleaching events. Two were global. One was local. Bleaching events are linked to very hot water.

What is bleaching? Corals have tiny algae living inside them that give them food. When the sea water gets too hot, the algae make harmful substances, and the coral pushes them out. Without the algae, the coral turns white and can die from a lack of energy.

“Now in a state of collapse.”Folha de S.Paulo, describing fire coral in Abrolhos

Consequences for nature: Fire coral nearly disappeared. Bahia brain coral fell by 45%. Fire coral is a nursery for fish and invertebrates. What this means for those animals is not known.

What causes climate change? People burn fossil fuels like coal, oil and gasoline in cars, power plants and factories. This puts extra carbon dioxide into the air. Gases like carbon dioxide trap heat, like a blanket around Earth. That makes Earth warmer, and the ocean warms too.

How do people depend on reefs? The reefs support fishing, tourism, jobs and the way coastal people make a living. Tourists also come to Abrolhos to watch humpback whales between July and November.

Solutions & Shared Responsibility

What is already being done?

  • The Abrolhos Marine National Park was created in 1983. It was Brazil’s first marine national park.
  • Protected areas help, but they have limits. Reports on this study say local protected areas did not stop the coral decline. Less than 2% of the Abrolhos Bank is fully protected, and some illegal fishing has been found inside the park.
  • In 2021, about 90 Brazilian scientists from 20 groups agreed on one way to check reefs for bleaching.
  • Scientists say cutting the gases that warm Earth is the main long-term way to help corals.

One useful step stands out. Scientists watched the reefs for 18 years. Their data tells us what changed, and when.

If people take action

No action to protect the reefs is described. Scientists did collect data from 2006 to 2023.

Other sources suggest these steps:

  • Cut the gases that warm Earth, so sea heatwaves become less common.
  • Keep and plant forests near the coast, so less soil washes into the sea.
  • Protect more of the sea, and stop illegal fishing.
  • Keep watching the reefs, so scientists can see what works.

If nothing is done

What will happen is not known. There are these clues:

  • Marine heatwaves have increased since 2010.
  • Fire coral was already low in number and then fell further.
  • “Weed” coral takes over reefs after more complex species die.

Journal prediction: What might happen to the Abrolhos reefs in ten years? Write your own prediction in your journal. Use two clues.

What Happened Next?

The study uses data only to 2023. Newer reports say coral cover on the Abrolhos reefs fell by about 15% over the 18 years. Large branching corals, which help build the reef, are being replaced by faster-growing corals that help other reef life less. In 2023 and 2024, another strong heatwave caused the fourth global bleaching event. It harmed reefs along Brazil’s coast, including the Abrolhos region. Scientists have not yet shared new counts for Abrolhos after 2023, so we do not know how much has changed since.
