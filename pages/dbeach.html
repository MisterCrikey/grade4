The Story of Bertioga

A beach 🏖️ is a strip of loose sand or small stones along the edge of the water. The water can be the sea, a big lake, or a river. Some beaches have pebbles 🪨 or shells 🐚 too. Waves 🌊 push the sand up and pull it back, so a beach is always changing.

Bertioga is a town on the coast of Brazil 🇧🇷, beside the Atlantic Ocean. It has about 33 kilometers (20 miles) of beaches that are good for swimming 🏊. Behind the beaches grows the Atlantic Forest 🌳, a thick green rainforest. About 64,000 people live in Bertioga today.

People lived here a very long time ago. About 4,000 years ago, early peoples left piles of shells and leftover food. These piles are called sambaquis. They show that people have lived by the sea for thousands of years. Later, the Tupi peoples lived here.

In 1531, a Portuguese ship ⛵ captain named Martim Afonso de Sousa landed here. In 1547, people built a stone fort 🏰 called Forte São João to guard the coast. It is the oldest fort in Brazil, and it is still in great shape. There were battles here between the Portuguese and the Tupinambá people. In 1563, the Tupinambá chief Cunhambebe visited the fort to make peace 🕊️. In 1565, ships left from here to start the city of Rio de Janeiro.

In the early 1700s, people hunted whales 🐋 here for their oil. The oil lit lamps 🪔 in cities like Santos and São Paulo. By 1940, Bertioga was just a small village of fishers 🎣. Then new roads 🛣️ and a ferry linked it to nearby towns. Visitors came to enjoy the beaches, and the village grew.

In 1944, Bertioga became part of the nearby city of Santos. But the people wanted to run their own town. On May 19, 1991, they voted 🗳️, and almost everyone said yes. There were 3,698 yes votes out of 3,925. That day is now the town’s birthday. Today, many visitors come in the summer ☀️ to enjoy the beaches.
