A Few Tree Species Lead the Amazon’s Comeback

A Brazilian study found that 15 to 25 kinds of trees do most of the work when cut-down rainforest grows back.

WhereBrazilian Amazon (reported from Rio de Janeiro, Brazil)
When18 August 2026
AuthorNot listed (published by France 24; the story is credited to AFP)
SourceFrance 24, Live news (AFP)

RIO DE JANEIRO, Brazil — Part of the Amazon rainforest can grow back after it has been destroyed. A Brazilian study found that only a few kinds of trees start this recovery. They can live in poor soil and strong sun, and they grow fast.

Past & Present: Comparing the Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a place where plants, animals and land work together. The Amazon is a very large rainforest ecosystem. It spans nine countries. Its billions of trees store vast amounts of carbon. Carbon is a natural material that trees keep in their wood and leaves. The Amazon’s trees are also a key buffer against global warming, the article says.

Biodiversity means the many kinds of living things in one place. The article says the Amazon has thousands of tree species. When a forest is removed, biodiversity is lost too.

The article describes two kinds of forest:

  • Mature, primary forests. Some species only live here. Shade is essential for most of them.
  • Recovering forests. A secondary forest is a forest that grows back on land that was cleared. The study says the Amazon has secondary forests about the size of Uruguay.

Here is what the article says about change over time:

  • In the past four decades, an area about the size of Spain was cleared in Brazil. This is data from Brazil’s National Institute for Space Research (INPE).
  • Today, deforestation is lower. Last year it fell to its lowest level in a decade.

Human Impact: What Changed and Why?

Deforestation means people remove a forest. The article says trees are “felled,” or cut down. This leaves a “complete rupture” with the environment, says Fernando Elias. He is a researcher at the Emilio Goeldi Museum of Para and led the study.

“You’re removing the forest’s ability to regulate the climate.”Fernando Elias, researcher, Emilio Goeldi Museum of Para

A human pressure is something people do that puts stress on nature. Here are the pressures the article names:

  • Cutting down forest, which removes biodiversity and carbon.
  • Using the land for pastures and farms. Secondary forests grow where these were left behind.
  • “Other human activities.” The article does not list them.

The article also reports how people are responding:

  • Brazil’s President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva wants to end forest loss by 2030.
  • Brazil has promised to restore 12 million hectares (46,300 square miles) of land.
  • Sometimes a landowner has a legal requirement to restore land.
Investigator’s noteThe article does not say who cut the trees or why most of the forest was cleared. It only names abandoned pastures and farms. Research question: What are the main reasons for deforestation in the Amazon? Check another source.

Climate Change & Consequences

The article talks about the forest and the climate. It does not say that climate change is harming the Amazon. It does give these facts:

  • The Amazon’s trees are a key buffer against global warming.
  • Trees store carbon. Removing a forest removes the carbon stored there.
  • Under the canopy of the core species, temperatures can be up to six degrees Celsius lower. The canopy is the top layer of leaves and branches.
  • Falling leaves add organic matter to the ground. Elias calls this “the beginning of forest recovery.”
Investigator’s noteThis article does not mention climate change as a cause of damage. It also does not say how people are affected. It only says that rural communities have long valued the babassu palm for its oil-rich nuts. Research question: How does climate change affect the Amazon today? Find another source.

Solutions & Shared Responsibility

The article gives these ideas and projects:

  • Study how forests grow back. This is key to understanding how to restore lost forests.
  • Find the pioneer species. These are the first trees to grow on cleared land. Elias says they have “the capacity for rapid colonization.”
  • Aim restoration at these species. Elias says this could make restoration faster.
  • Follow Brazil’s goals: end forest loss by 2030 and restore 12 million hectares.
  • Join science projects. The study was part of the Sustainable Amazon Network, an international effort.
“It’s fantastic because the forest is resilient.”Fernando Elias, researcher, Emilio Goeldi Museum of Para

The study used data from four regions of eastern Amazonia. It looked at more than 25,000 trees and palms. The forests were from one to 60 years old. Trees named in the article include Cecropia palmata, Inga alba, the Inaja palm and the babassu palm.

If people take action

  • A small group of species starts forest recovery.
  • These trees create shade and add leaves to the soil.
  • Brazil’s deforestation fell to its lowest level in a decade last year.
  • Knowing the key species can help make restoration faster.

If nothing is done

The article does not say what will happen. It gives clues:

  • Removing a forest removes biodiversity and carbon.
  • It also removes the forest’s ability to regulate the climate.
