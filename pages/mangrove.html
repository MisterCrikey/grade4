Saving Ecuador’s Mangrove Forests

Mangroves 🌿 are special trees that grow in salty water near the coast. Ecuador 🇪🇨 has some of the tallest mangrove forests in the Americas.

These trees help people. Crabs 🦀 and fish 🐟 live in them, and families catch food there. The trees also act like a wall 🛡️ that keeps floods away from towns.

But since 1969, Ecuador has lost almost one quarter of its mangroves. Many were cleared to make room for shrimp 🦐 farms, other farms and growing cities.

There is good news. Ecuador has strong rules to protect mangroves. Some forests are in protected areas, and local families look after others. In these places, far fewer trees get cut down.

A new report says some of the old mangrove land can grow back. People can plant 🌱 new trees there. Money, teamwork and careful watching will help the forests come back.

Original story: Wetlands International, October 26, 2026
