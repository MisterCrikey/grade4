Fewer Trees Cut Down: Brazil Reports a Record Fall in Amazon Forest Loss

The Amazon is the world’s largest rainforest. Brazil’s government says forest clearing dropped sharply, just before an important election.

WhereBrazil (Amazon rainforest and Cerrado savanna)
When17 October 2026
AuthorIone Wells and Joel Guinto
SourceBBC News (Latin America)

BRAZIL — Brazil’s government says less forest is being cut down in the Amazon. Satellite data shows that in the year to July 2026, the rate of forest loss fell by 41.8%. That is the biggest drop since records began in 1988. The news comes before a presidential election run-off, where the future of the rainforest is a key issue.

Past & Present: Comparing the Ecosystem

The Amazon is the world’s largest rainforest. Sixty percent of it is in Brazil. Tropical rainforests like the Amazon are home to millions of species. A species is one kind of living thing. The Cerrado is also in Brazil. It is Brazil’s savanna region. A savanna is a grassy land with few trees.

“Absolutely stratospheric.”Joao Paulo Capobianco, Brazil’s Environment Minister, on the fall in forest loss

Then: From 2019 to 2022, The Amazon lost nearly 60% more forest than in the four years before. This comes from official data reported by the news agency AFP.

Then, again: In 2022, deforestation fell to a six-year low. Deforestation means cutting down or clearing forest.

Now: In the year to July 2026, forest loss fell 41.8% in the Amazon. In the Cerrado, it fell 23.8%.

Who lives in the Amazon? Many animals live in the Amazon rainforest. Jaguars, harpy eagles and pink river dolphins live there. So do sloths, black spider monkeys, macaws and poison dart frogs. More than 70% of the world’s jaguars live in the Amazon. Researchers reported in 2019 that about 17% of the Amazon had been cleared in the past 50 years, mostly to make room for cattle.

Human Impact: What Changed and Why?

People took actions that harmed the forest:

  • Land clearing. Tropical rainforests have come under growing pressure from decades of deliberate land clearance. A pressure is something that pushes against a living place. Here, people cleared land for farming and logging.
  • Mining. Previous governments promoted mining in indigenous lands in the Amazon.
  • Fewer protections. Forest clearing soared while resources to protect the forest were cut.

Then policy changed. The current administration reversed his predecessor’s policies. He tightened environmental monitoring. He also tightened penalties for illegal logging.

Why do people clear forest for farming? Cattle ranching is the biggest reason. Cattle pastures cover about 80% of the land cleared in the Amazon. People also clear forest to grow crops such as soy, and for logging, mining, roads and dams. Ranchers often use fire to clear the land.

Climate Change & Consequences

The Amazon is a crucial buffer in the world’s fight against climate change. A buffer is something that softens a problem. A healthy rainforest absorbs vast amounts of carbon dioxide, a gas that warms the planet. This helps keep the Earth cool.

Rainforests are also under pressure from climate change. Scientists warn that climate change can create conditions for wildfires.

“With political will, it is possible to curb deforestation and greenhouse gas emissions.”Marcio Astrini, executive secretary, Observatorio do Clima

What causes climate change? People burn fossil fuels like coal, oil and gasoline in cars, power plants and factories. This puts extra carbon dioxide into the air. Cutting down forests adds carbon dioxide too. These gases trap heat around Earth, like a blanket.

How can forest loss change rainfall? Trees pull water from the soil and release it into the air through their leaves. That moist air helps make clouds and rain. When many trees are cut down, this cycle breaks. Less rain can fall, and drought can follow.

Why are drought and fire a danger? In 2023, a severe drought hit the Amazon. Scientists said climate change made that drought about 30 times more likely. Rivers fell so low that hundreds of communities that travel by river were cut off. Dry forest also burns more easily, and fires can spread from cleared land into standing forest.

Solutions & Shared Responsibility

  • A promise. When the current Government took office, he pledged to end deforestation in the Amazon by 2030. A pledge is a serious promise. On social media he wrote: “Our goal is to achieve zero illegal deforestation by 2030.”
  • Watching and penalties. Brazilian government tightened environmental monitoring and penalties for illegal logging.
  • Measuring. A satellite is a machine in space. Satellite data shows the forest loss figures.
  • Voters. The rainforest’s future is a key issue in the run-off on 25 October.

If people take action

  • Deforestation fell 41.8% in the Amazon in a year.
  • It was the biggest drop since records began in 1988.
  • Astrini calls it an “historic achievement” for Brazil.

If nothing is done

What will happen is not known. There are these clues:

  • When protection resources were cut, forest clearing soared.
  • Under previous the goverment, the Amazon lost nearly 60% more forest than before.

Journal prediction: What might happen to the Amazon if protection stops? Write your own prediction in your journal. Use two clues.

What Happened Next?

The future of the Brazilian Amazon depends on protecting the trees that are still standing before too much of the forest is lost to recover.
