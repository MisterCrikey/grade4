The Amazon Has a Long History

The Amazon is a giant rainforest 🌳 in South America 🌎. Many people think almost nobody ever lived there. They picture wild trees and animals, and that is all. But that idea is wrong. People have lived in the Amazon for thousands of years, and the forest has a long history.

Indigenous peoples, the first peoples of this land, speak about 170 languages 🗣️ in Brazil today. In all the Amazon countries together, there are about 300. That tells us many different peoples live in the forest. Each one has its own words and its own way of life.

The forest peoples were also great farmers 🧑‍🌾. Cassava 🥔 and cocoa 🍫 were first grown in the Amazon. People all over the world eat them today. About 5,500 years ago, people in a place now called Rondônia began making dark, rich soil 🟤. It is called terra preta, which means “dark earth.” It covers about 2% of the Amazon, and farmers still use it to grow food.

About 2,500 years ago, people began building big things. They dug ditches ⛏️ and made roads 🛣️ and mounds ⛰️ of earth. Some lived in large towns, almost like cities. They made beautiful pots 🏺 and stone objects. You can see some of them today in museums 🏛️.

When Europeans arrived, about 8 to 10 million people lived in the Amazon. Sadly, many died in the early years from sickness 🤒, war, and being forced to work. Later, in the 1700s, scientists 🔬 traveled through the forest. The land looked empty, and the old towns were hidden under trees. There were no stone buildings, so they thought no one had ever lived there.

But the Amazon does have a history. Its peoples helped make the forest what it is. Without them, the Amazon would not be what it is today, and it would not be in the future.
