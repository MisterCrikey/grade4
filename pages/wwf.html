Restinga formation in São Paulo State may receive protected area

fevereiro, 04 2010

Diagnosis made by WWF-Brazil contributed to the proposal by the Atlantic Forest Programme and confirmed the importance of the area of 8,025 hectares, which can save one of the most threatened ecosystems of the Atlantic Forest.
One of the most important areas of Atlantic Forest in the state of São Paulo may receive a new protected area. The proposed protected area of 8,025 hectares is in the municipality of Bertioga and should help to reinforce the protection of part of the São Paulo Restinga ecosystem which is home to a rich biodiversity and vulnerable to pressure from real estate interests and tourism. In addition to protecting rivers that provide the region’s water supply and threatened species that are exclusive to that particular Biome, the conservation will be the first one to safeguard part of the plain that skirts the lower slopes of the Serra do Mar State Park mountains.

The definition of the area was based on a set of criteria such as the characteristics of the wildlife found there, the vegetation and the water resources and the process received the support of WFF-Brasil’s Atlantic Forest Programme, which, together with partner organisations Forest Foundation and the São Paulo Forestry Institute, undertook a set of studies in  an area of 10,393 hectares. The so-called ‘Socio-economic, Environmental and Cultural Diagnosis of the Bertioga Polygon’ involved researchers with a variety of specialised qualifications  and revealed that the place has rich biological diversity and protects rivers that form three sub-basins – Una, Itaguaré and Guaratuba - , that are essential to the region.

“Creating and managing protected areas, especially in places where large populations are concentrated faces the challenge of  establishing harmony between human beings and the environment and that harmony means a better quality of life for people” declares Atlantic Forest Programme coordinator, Luciana Simões.

The proposal for setting up the  protected area is already being discussed with various sectors of society. From December 4 to 7, 2009 four public meetings were held to prepare the final proposal that will be officially put forward at a later date at a public hearing to be announced in the Official Gazette and in other media. The public hearing is expected to take place some time in February 2010.

Studies provide supporting elements for the creation of  protected areas

The choosing of the site served as a pilot process to test criteria that can help to ensure protection for habitats with the highest level of representativity thus avoiding gaps in the conservation of species of flora and fauna, and of ecological processes. The aim of the methodology was to guarantee that the areas would be chosen on the basis of technical parameters that confirm their importance and no longer on the basis of circumstantial opportunities, a process that is still predominant.
.

“History shows us that most parks were created because of certain opportunities that existed but that should no longer be the way because it does not fulfil the objective of preserving biodiversity. We want to get away from opportunism and the old ways of creating parks in a random manner”, stressed Atlantic Forest Programme Coordinator, Luciana Simões.

A bio-diverse heritage on the São Paulo coast

The Restinga formations extend along almost the entire length of the Brazilian coastline and form an intricate complex of plants and animals adapted to maritime influences and the sandy soils. Currently there is only one  protected area that encompasses this habitat in the São Paulo state system.

Data gathered under the auspices of the Socio-economic, Environmental and Cultural Diagnosis of the Bertioga Polygon conducted by WWF-Brasil, underscore the need to protect the remaining portion of Restinga formation. Even though they were not the object of such intense studies as certain other  protected areas, two particular areas within the region were identified as the stretches of Atlantic Forest endowed with greatest richness of species in the state of São Paulo especially in regard to the diversity of amphibians  - the greatest diversity known for this biome – and the concentration of threatened bird species and also, in regard to the flora, the very large numbers of orchid species.

Other studies undertaken in the Serra do Mar State Park also point to the need to preserve environments on the coastal plain. The forest formations on the Bertioga lowlands, those at altitudes of 0 to 50 metres above sea level, are classified as Important Bird Areas – IBA and of great importance for the conservation of bird species. According to Birdlife International / SAVE Brasil such locations are critically important for the long-term conservation of birds.

The researchers identified 1,000 plant species in the Bertioga polygon. That corresponds to 13% of all the species to be found in the entire state in vaster and more intensely studied areas. Of the species identified 44 are threatened with extinction and another 17 types of vegetation are endemic, that is they only exist in the states of Rio de Janeiro and São Paulo.

The Bertioga polygon includes parts of the lower slopes of the mountains of the Serra do Mar State Park so that it functions as a biodiversity corridor  connecting to the highlands in addition to protecting a network of bodies of water that feed three river sub-basins on the coastal plain where the remaining stands continue to suffer great pressure.

Data on the proposed protected area

Representativity
  • Restinga ecosystem, the most threatened area of Atlantic Forest in the state of São Paulo
Location
  • Central coastland of the State of São Paulo in the municipality of Bertioga
Size
  • 8,025 hectares
Water
  • Two river sub-basins – Itaguaré and Guaratuba rivers
Biodiversity
  • 40 species of amphibians and 53 species of reptiles
  • 25 species of large and medium-sized mammals
  • 6 species of mammals threatened with extinction
  • Considered to be an Important Bird Area vital for the conservation of bird species
  • Around 1,000 species of plants
  • 44 species of the flora are under threat of extinction
  • 17 types of endemic vegetation only found in the States of São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro
