The Desert That Burst Into Flowers

The Atacama Desert 🏜️ in Chile 🇨🇱 is one of the driest places on Earth. Some parts get almost no rain for years.

This year, heavy rain 🌧️ soaked the sand. Seeds and bulbs that had been asleep underground for a very long time woke up.

Soon, millions of flowers 🌸 covered the desert in pink, yellow and purple. More than 200 kinds of plants bloomed. It was the biggest flower show in 30 years!

One star flower is the garra de león, which means “lion’s claw.” It is bright red, and it grows only on a small part of Chile’s coast. Lizards 🦎, bats and guanacos 🦙 come to the bloom, too.

Scientists 🔬 are collecting seeds 🌱 to keep them safe in a seed bank. The flowers last only a few weeks. Then they drop their seeds, and the seeds wait for the next big rain.

Desert plants are clever. Some have tiny hairs or waxy skin that help them hold on to water 💧. Lots of visitors 📸 came to see the show.
