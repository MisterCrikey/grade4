A Super Dry Place

The Atacama desert 🏜️ is a long, thin strip of land in the country of Chile 🇨🇱. It sits between the Pacific Ocean 🌊 and the Andes Mountains ⛰️. The strip is almost 1,000 kilometers long. That is about 600 miles. It is one of the driest deserts by the sea anywhere on Earth.

Some parts get only about 1 millimeter of rain 🌧️ in a whole year. That is hardly any water at all! So very few plants 🌱 can grow. But fog 🌫️ drifts in from the ocean and settles on the hills. A little light rain in winter helps too. About 550 kinds of plants live here, and many of them grow nowhere else.

The middle of the desert looks like another world. Scientists have used it to test early versions of Moon 🌙 landers and Mars 🔴 rovers. The dry, empty ground is a lot like those faraway places. It is so dry that dead plants do not rot. Some have stayed there for thousands of years!

Very few animals can live here. A few scorpions 🦂 and insects hide in the sand. Lizards 🦎 and a small songbird 🐦 hunt them. Darwin’s leaf-eared mouse 🐭 is a tiny hero. It gets water from the seeds and fruits it eats! A gray fox 🦊 sometimes passes through too.

People have changed this land too. In the 1980s, the price of copper 🟠 went up. So mining ⛏️ started up again. The mines use a strong acid, and no one has really studied how it hurt life on land and in the sea. New roads 🛣️ for the mines also brought more people to live here. Cities growing, farm animals eating plants, and cutting wood for fires have caused more trouble.

Now people are working to protect the desert. Pan de Azúcar National Park 🏞️ is the big protected area here. Experts want it to grow north to guard more of the special plants. They also want less mining and safe paths so animals can cross the roads. Some of these plants may even help scientists learn to grow better crops 🌾 someday.
